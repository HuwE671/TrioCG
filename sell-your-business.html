Sell your business, on your terms.

Whether you’re planning your retirement or ready for something new, we offer a direct, confidential route to sale with a buyer who intends to look after what you’ve built.

Why owners choose to sell to us

Most small business owners only sell once. We try to make that experience as clear and calm as possible.

You speak directly to the buyer

No chain of intermediaries and no auction. The people you meet are the people who make the decision.

Confidential from day one

We sign an NDA before you share figures. Your business is never advertised, and nobody is told until you’re ready.

Your team stays

We buy businesses because of the people who run them. Keeping your team together is part of our plan, not an afterthought.

A structure that suits you

Upfront payment, a phased exit or a longer handover. We build the deal around your plans and how involved you want to stay.

Your name and reputation continue

We keep the business name, the customer relationships and the way you do things, and build from there.

Honest updates throughout

We tell you plainly where things stand at every stage, including anything that might affect the timetable or price.

What happens after the sale

The questions owners ask us most often aren’t about price. They’re about the people and the business they’re leaving behind.

Your employees

Their jobs transfer with the business on their existing terms. We’ll meet them with you and invest in their development.

Your customers

We plan introductions with you so relationships continue smoothly, with the same service they’ve always had.

The business

It keeps its name and identity, with the added backing of a wider group for investment in systems, marketing and people.

You

You step back when you’re ready. Your role after completion is agreed upfront and written into the deal.

Questions owners ask us

If your question isn’t here, ask us directly. There’s no such thing as a silly question when you’re selling something you’ve spent years building.

Ask us a question

How do you decide what my business is worth?

We look mainly at sustainable profit: what the business reliably earns once one-off costs and owner-specific expenses are adjusted for. We also consider cash flow, how dependent the business is on you personally, customer concentration, the strength of the team and future prospects. When we make an offer, we explain how we arrived at it.

What information will I need to share?

At the start, nothing more than a conversation. Once a non-disclosure agreement is in place, we’ll usually ask for the last three years of accounts, recent management figures and an overview of the team, customers and suppliers. If your records aren’t perfect, that’s common in smaller businesses and we can work through it with you.

Will my staff, customers or suppliers find out?

Not unless you decide they should. We treat every conversation as confidential and sign an NDA before you share any figures. We agree together when and how the team and key customers are told, which is usually close to or at completion.

Do I have to stay on after the sale?

That’s up to you and part of what we agree in the offer. Some owners want a short handover of a few weeks; others prefer to stay on part-time or in an advisory role for longer. Where a business relies heavily on the owner, a longer transition protects its value for everyone.

How are deals usually structured?

Every deal is different. Payment may be made in full at completion, or split between an upfront amount and later payments. We’ll set out the structure clearly and encourage you to take advice from your own accountant on the tax position.

How long does a sale take?

For businesses of this size, completing a sale typically takes a few months from agreeing an offer, depending mainly on how quickly information can be gathered and how the legal work progresses. We’ll give you a realistic timetable at the offer stage.

Do I need my own advisers?

Yes. We recommend you appoint an accountant and a solicitor with experience of business sales. They’ll protect your interests, and in our experience it makes the whole process smoother for both sides.

My business is outside Hampshire. Should I still get in touch?

Our focus is Hampshire, Surrey, West Sussex, Dorset and Berkshire. If you’re just outside these counties, we’re still happy to talk.

I’m not ready to sell yet. Is it too early to talk?

Not at all. Many owners get in touch one to three years before they want to sell. An early conversation gives you time to prepare and often leads to a better outcome.

Thinking about selling in the next few years?

The earlier we talk, the more options you have. A first conversation is confidential, informal and carries no obligation.
